What is a Business Problem?

Direct Answer:
A business problem is a specific situation or challenge that prevents a business from achieving its goals or negatively affects its performance, customers, revenue, or operations.

Business problems can affect different areas of an organization, such as sales, customer service, marketing, finance, employees, operations, or technology.

Example:
If a company's sales are continuously decreasing, the decline in sales may indicate a business problem that needs further investigation.

Business Problem vs Symptom

What is a Business Problem?

A business problem is the underlying challenge that negatively affects a company's goals, performance, customers, revenue, or operations.

What is a Symptom?

A symptom is an observable sign or result that indicates that a problem may exist. A symptom does not always identify the actual underlying cause.

Example:

Symptom: Sales are decreasing.

Possible underlying problem: Customers may be switching to competitors, product demand may have changed, or the business may have an issue with pricing or customer experience.

The actual business problem should be identified by collecting information and investigating the possible causes rather than assuming that the symptom itself is the root cause.

How to Identify the Root Cause of a Business Problem

What is a Root Cause?
A root cause is an underlying reason that contributes to a business problem. Finding the root cause helps a business choose a more effective solution.

Steps to Identify the Root Cause

  1. Define the problem clearly
    Describe exactly what is happening and how it affects the business.
  2. Collect facts and data
    Look at relevant information such as sales, customer feedback, costs, processes, or performance data.
  3. Identify the symptoms
    Separate the visible signs of the problem from the underlying causes.
  4. Ask "Why?"
    Continue asking why the problem is occurring to explore deeper causes.
  5. Investigate possible causes
    Consider factors related to customers, products, employees, processes, technology, pricing, or the market.
  6. Verify the evidence
    Check whether the available data supports the suspected cause.
  7. Choose a solution
    Once the underlying cause is better understood, develop and test an appropriate solution.
Example:

A company notices that sales are declining.

Instead of immediately assuming that the problem is "low sales", the business can investigate customer feedback, pricing, competitors, product demand, marketing performance, and other relevant factors.

The actual cause should be verified using evidence before deciding on a solution.
